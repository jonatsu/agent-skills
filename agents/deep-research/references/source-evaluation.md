# Evaluating Sources and Stating Confidence

## Primary First

A primary source is the one that made a claim first: the specification, the patent, the source code, the datasheet,
the paper, or the maintainer's own statement. Prefer it to anything that summarizes it. When only a summary is
reachable, say so beside the claim.

## Independence

Two sources are independent only when neither repeats the other and they do not share an owner or an incentive. Judge
independence by the evidence behind each source, not by how many domains carry it. A press release and the articles
that reword it count as one origin, and so do a vendor's documentation and its blog. A project's own records describe
the project but do not validate it.

## Warning Signs

Read more carefully, and look for the primary source, when a source shows any of these:

- speculation presented as fact, in words such as "could", "may", "is expected to", or a projection;
- an aggregator or content farm instead of the original;
- authority claimed without evidence, or "it is reported" with no one named;
- marketing language, or data picked to support a conclusion;
- no date, or a date far older than the subject's current state.

## Conflicting Sources

Report a conflict as a finding. Prefer the source that is more recent, more primary, and more consistent with facts
already established, and say which of these decided it. When none decides it, report both claims and what would settle
the question.

## Confidence

Give each major claim a confidence level, and state its basis: the kind, amount, and quality of the evidence, and how
far the sources agree.

| Level    | Basis                                                                                |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| High     | Several independent primary sources agree, and none conflicts                        |
| Moderate | One primary source, or several secondary sources that agree, or a minor conflict     |
| Low      | One secondary source, an unresolved conflict, or an inference from indirect evidence |

A well-established fact can stand without a level. Confidence describes the evidence, not a probability.

When the question itself asks how likely something is, answer with these terms, and keep the confidence level in a
separate sentence:

| Term             | Probability |
| ---------------- | ----------- |
| Almost no chance | 1 to 5%     |
| Very unlikely    | 5 to 20%    |
| Unlikely         | 20 to 45%   |
| Roughly even     | 45 to 55%   |
| Likely           | 55 to 80%   |
| Very likely      | 80 to 95%   |
| Almost certain   | 95 to 99%   |
